Three of the backgrounds read a picture instead of a formula. image shows it, ascii redraws it out of the charset, and mask uses it as a stencil that text and motion are only allowed to appear inside. All three take the picture from a slot, and there are five slots.

Nothing here is uploaded. The word is wrong and kept only because it is the word people look for. A picture you choose is decoded in this tab and written to IndexedDB on your own machine; no request carries it anywhere, and the server has no route that would accept one if it tried.

the ones that ship

Two pictures are offered as one-click samples, so there is something to point the programs at without going and finding a file. Both are drawn for the job:

sphere   one shape, and every tone between black and white
         inside it. what `ascii` wants.
tree     one hard-edged silhouette. what `mask` wants — an
         outline still legible when it is twenty rows tall.

The other two are borrowed from elsewhere on the site, and they are here as the counter-example. life is a frame of Conway's life; phyllotaxis is a vector spiral. Both look like something at full size and come out as almost nothing at twenty rows — sparse dots average to grey, and thin lines disappear between the cells. That is the whole constraint these programs have and it is worth seeing fail: a picture here needs large shapes and a tonal range, not fine detail.

Those two are linked to look at, not offered as buttons: a one-click sample that renders nothing is a trap, and they are the reason this page says any of this. A slot with nothing in it falls back to the sphere rather than to nothing, which is why the playground shows the picture programs doing something instead of two blank rectangles. Put your own picture in a slot and that stops.

what to expect

ascii   wants contrast. a flat photograph turns to mud —
        push `ascii contrast` up until the shapes separate.
mask    wants a subject. it draws only where the picture is
        bright, so a dark photograph masks almost everything
        away. invert it, or drop the threshold.

The knobs are on their own page: which slot, how hard to read it, what text to pour into the stencil. The playground shows every program at once and is the fastest way to see whether a picture is worth keeping.
