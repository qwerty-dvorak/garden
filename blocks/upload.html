Three of the backgrounds read a picture instead of a formula. image shows it, ascii redraws it out of the charset, and mask uses it as a stencil that text is only allowed to appear inside.

Pick a picture, then press one of the three. Each opens it on a page with nothing else on it, because a background shown behind an essay is a background you cannot see.

Nothing here is uploaded. The word is wrong and kept only because it is the word people look for. A picture you choose is decoded in this tab and written to IndexedDB on your own machine; no request carries it anywhere, and the server has no route that would accept one. That is also why a link you send someone carries the dials but not the picture — a shared ascii draws against whatever the person opening it has.

the two that ship

sphere is one shape with every tone between black and white inside it, which is what ascii wants — a subject that uses the whole ramp. tree is one hard-edged silhouette, which is what mask wants — an outline still legible when it is twenty rows tall.

Both are drawn for the job. Two pictures already on the site were tried first and neither worked: life is sparse dots that average to near-black at twenty rows, and phyllotaxis is thin lines that vanish between the cells. Not one cell of either cleared the stencil threshold. A picture here needs large shapes and a tonal range, not fine detail — which is worth knowing before you go looking for one of your own.

what to expect

ascii   wants contrast. a flat photograph turns to mud —
        push `ascii contrast` up until the shapes separate.
mask    wants a subject. it draws only where the picture is
        bright, so a dark photograph masks almost everything
        away. invert it, or drop the threshold.

Five slots, the rest of the dials, and the gallery are on the knobs page. The playground runs every program at once and is the fastest way to see whether a picture is worth keeping.
